\Large\textbf{Plus Disease Detection in Retinopathy of Prematurity}\\[0.2cm]
\large\textbf{Vascular Biomarkers and Frozen Image Representations for \lr{Plus} Disease Detection}\\[0.8cm]
\Large\textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}

\noindent
Retinopathy of prematurity is a leading cause of preventable childhood blindness, and treatment decisions depend on recognising \lr{Plus} disease. That recognition rests on expert visual assessment of fundus images and remains observer-dependent near its boundaries. This project asks whether five scalar vascular biomarkers separate \lr{Plus} on their own, and whether adding them to a learned image representation provides an incremental contribution.

Three components were built. The measurement component computes five vascular biomarkers with field-of-view-aware definitions and a content-area coverage denominator. The representation component provides two frozen encoders, an RGB representation from EfficientNet-B5 and a vessel-map representation from EfficientNet-B4. Eight primary models were built from these components, alongside secondary neural models. The cohort contains 8{,}862 fundus images from 414 groups across three acquisition sources, split by group so that no group appears in more than one partition.

Adding the spatial vessel representation to the image representation produced a supported improvement in ranking performance and in probability quality, and the increment replicated under source-held-out evaluation, which uses no target-source information of any kind, and also with a different RGB architecture. The five scalar biomarkers produced no supported incremental contribution in any tested regime; used alone they ranked well above chance yet below every image-based model.

Cross-source performance is markedly lower than pooled-test performance, no expert reference mask exists for this cohort and the \lr{Plus} source supports only the restricted \lr{Normal}-versus-\lr{Plus} comparison, so these results demonstrate ranking ability against the recorded label and are not evidence of clinical readiness, not a replacement for expert assessment, and not externally validated.
